一、本周核心趋势1. Key Trends This Week

  • CTV 正从“可投放渠道”升级为“可衡量、可自动化”的核心视频媒介，行业开始按剧集、活动和品牌安全等更细粒度优化投放。例如 PubMatic 与 ThinkResult 推出亚太首个 agentic CTV 活动，FreeWheel 上线剧集级报告，Walmart Connect 通过收购 Vibe 补强 CTV 布局。
  • AI 正从单点功能变成广告平台的操作层，规划、创意、执行和衡量被逐步并入统一工作流，买方与平台都在追求更高自动化。 例如 StackAdapt 推出 Ivy Studio，Microsoft Advertising 强化 Copilot，Adjust 对接 OpenAI Ads Manager，均指向同一方向。
  • 品牌安全与内容可信度正在成为新竞争门槛，随着 AI 内容和新兴社交流量扩张，第三方验证与适配性测量被前置到投放链路。 例如 TikTok Pangle 接入 DV 和 IAS，IAS 为 Threads 推出 AI 品牌安全测量，DoubleVerify 直接警示低质 AI 内容风险。
  • 零售媒体正从站内流量售卖转向跨渠道测量与商业化基础设施，广告主不只要买库存，更要看归因、ROI 和多屏扩展。 例如 Singular 加入 Universal Ads MMP 计划，InMobi 为 Glance 提供 MMP 集成，Kroger/Instacart 的 ROI 讨论和 Walmart Connect 的 CTV 扩张都在强化这一点。
  • CTV is moving from a buyable channel to a measurable, increasingly automated video medium, with finer-grained optimization becoming the norm. For example, PubMatic and ThinkResult launched APAC’s first agentic CTV campaign, FreeWheel introduced episode-level reporting, and Walmart Connect reinforced its CTV push via the Vibe acquisition.
  • AI is becoming the operating layer of ad platforms, unifying planning, creative, execution, and measurement into one workflow. For example, StackAdapt launched Ivy Studio, Microsoft Advertising expanded Copilot capabilities, and Adjust integrated with OpenAI Ads Manager.
  • Brand safety and content trust are becoming harder requirements as AI-generated content and new surfaces expand risk. For example, TikTok Pangle partnered with DoubleVerify and IAS, IAS launched AI-driven brand safety measurement for Threads, and DoubleVerify warned that low-quality AI content weakens brand trust.
  • Retail media is shifting from onsite inventory selling to cross-channel measurement infrastructure and commercial operating systems. For example, Singular joined Universal Ads’ MMP program, InMobi added MMP integrations for Glance, and Kroger/Instacart ROI discussions plus Walmart Connect’s CTV expansion all point in the same direction.

Publishers can use a single-sign-on service or a partnering third-party ID provider (like LiveRamp) to capture user IDs or integrate UID2 API directly into their login system, then get a UID2 token and pass it to an SSP.Supply-side platforms (SSPs) will pass the UID2 token to a DSP in a bid request. The idea: have a mechanism where SSPs and DSPs can use a decrypted UID2 token like any other identifier such as a cookie.Demand-side platforms (DSPs) install a UID2 RTB SDK Client to decrypt tokens. Then register with UID2 Administrator to receive an authkey used to initialize the SDK. Once done, a DSP can call the SDK during RTB auctions to decrypt a UID2 token into a UID2 identifier. The SDK sends over the UID2 along with a timestamp of when a user first established the UID2 — and notifies if the token is expired. This mechanism allows DSPs to match audience data and conduct frequency capping. The DSP will also need to install a mechanism for honoring user opt-out requests, sent by the UID2 Administrator.Data management platforms (DMPs) and data providers will have to supply audience data based on UID2 to DSPs. The mechanics are similar to the way that data providers push data sets using a cookie or device ID. The industry is shifting toward first-party data strategies and contextual advertising as the practical path forward.
