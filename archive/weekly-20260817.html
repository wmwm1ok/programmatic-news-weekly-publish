  • Programmatic advertising is the automated buying and placing of digital ads using software and data, instead of manual deals. It targets specific audiences across websites, apps, video, and connected TV, buying each impression individually in milliseconds based on who will see it. The big shift is from buying ad space to buying audiences, which makes spending precise and easy to scale. Most display, video, and CTV ads you see today are bought this way, as a page loads.What is real-time bidding (RTB)?Real-time bidding is the live auction that powers most programmatic advertising. When someone opens a page with ad space, the publisher sends that impression to an ad exchange, advertisers' platforms check it against their targeting and bid in real time. The exchange picks the winner and the ad loads, in under 100 milliseconds. Advertisers never pick one website; they set rules, and the system buys the matching impressions. It is how billions of ad slots are sold daily.What is the difference between a DSP and an SSP?They sit on opposite sides of the same marketplace. A demand-side platform, or DSP, is the advertiser's tool: it buys ad impressions across many exchanges from one place, using your targeting and budgets. A supply-side platform, or SSP, is the publisher's tool: it offers ad space to many buyers and gets the best price per impression. The exchange is where the two meet. So advertisers buy through a DSP, publishers sell through an SSP, and the exchange connects them.How much does programmatic advertising cost?
